SLIDE 7
Identity
"Who am I?"
(Walk slowly toward the audience.)
Now we've arrived at what I believe is the most important question every person will ever answer.
Not...
"What do I do?"
Not...
"Where do I work?"
Not...
"How much money do I make?"
But rather...
"Who am I?"
Because everything else grows from the answer to that question.
Most people spend years introducing themselves by what they do.
"I'm a teacher."
"I'm a business owner."
"I'm an engineer."
"I'm retired."
"I'm a parent."
Those are roles.
They are important.
But they are not your identity.
Roles can change.
Jobs change.
Titles change.
Careers change.
Life seasons change.
But identity remains.
God never introduced David by saying...
"This is Israel's future king."
He first introduced David as...
"A man after My own heart."
Think about that.
Long before David ever wore a crown...
God had already established who David was.
The throne didn't create David's identity.
His identity prepared him for the throne.
That changes how we view success.
Many people believe...
"When I finally achieve success...
then I'll become somebody."
God says...
"No.
First become the person...
and then I'll entrust you with greater responsibility."
That is why character always precedes influence.

"What long-term work fulfills my purpose?"
One of the greatest discoveries you can make...

People often confuse the two.
Purpose answers...
Why do I exist?

What lifelong work fulfills that purpose?
Here's another way to think about it.

Mission is the vehicle.
Purpose is the reason.
Mission is the expression.
For example...

"To bring hope to hurting families."

writing books...
creating educational programs...
mentoring couples...

Nehemiah illustrates this beautifully.
His heart was broken because Jerusalem's walls were in ruins.

His mission was restoring God's people.
The wall simply became the vehicle.
Likewise...
Solomon's mission wasn't merely constructing a building.
His mission was establishing a place where God's presence would be honored among His people.
The Temple expressed the mission.
The mission expressed God's purpose.

SLIDE 17
Action
Where mission becomes strategy
Up to this point, we have focused on identity and direction.
We have asked:
Who am I?
Why am I here?
What is my mission?
What future am I building?
What assignment has God given me in this season?
But now we come to the point where many people get stuck.
They have insight.
They have inspiration.
They may even have a strong sense of calling.
But they do not have a plan.
And without a plan, inspiration begins to fade.
This is where mission must become strategy.
Vision must become structure.
Purpose must become action.
A dream may be powerful, but a dream without movement remains only an idea.
That is why this third zone is called Action.
This zone contains three important levels:
A life-changing project.
A strategic goal.
And a milestone.
These three levels help us move from broad direction to measurable progress.
Let me say this clearly:
God can give the vision.
God can open the door.
God can provide the opportunity.
But we still have a responsibility to organize the work.
Faith is not an excuse for disorder.
Prayer is not a replacement for planning.
Spiritual sensitivity and strategic thinking belong together.
Nehemiah prayed.
But he also inspected the walls.
He organized the workers.
He assigned responsibilities.
He prepared for opposition.
He tracked progress.
He combined dependence on God with disciplined execution.
That is the pattern we are about to explore.

SLIDE 18
Life-Changing Project
What major initiative advances my mission?
A life-changing project is a major initiative that significantly advances your mission.
This is larger than an ordinary task.
It is larger than a short-term assignment.
It is something that can reshape your life, the lives of others, or both.
Examples might include:
Writing a book.
Launching a nonprofit.
Building a school.
Starting a ministry.
Creating a community program.
Developing a business that solves a real problem.
Completing a degree that opens a new direction.
Rebuilding a family relationship.
Creating a platform that helps others transform their lives.
These projects often take months or years.
They usually require courage.
They require resources.
They require sacrifice.
And almost always, they require growth.
The project changes others, but it also changes you.
Noah did not simply complete a construction project.
He built an ark in obedience to God during a time when the surrounding culture had become deeply corrupt.
The ark preserved life.
It protected the covenant.
It became a physical expression of obedience.
Nehemiah did not merely repair a wall.
The wall represented restored dignity.
Restored security.
Restored identity.
Restored worship.
The physical project carried spiritual and communal meaning.
That is what makes a project life-changing.
The visible work is connected to a deeper mission.
________________________________________
Practical Distinction
A regular project might be:
Create a website.
A life-changing project might be:
Build an online platform that helps thousands of people discover purpose and make a life transition.
A regular project might be:
Write a workbook.
A life-changing project might be:
Develop a complete transformation curriculum that equips people to rebuild their lives.
The difference is not only size.
The difference is mission.

SLIDE 19
Strategic Goal
What measurable result must be achieved?
A strategic goal turns a broad project into a measurable result.
This is where clarity becomes essential.
A goal must have a finish line.
You should be able to determine whether it has been achieved.
Not vaguely.
Objectively.
For example:
“Work on my book” is not a strong strategic goal.
“Complete and publish a 120-page book by October 31” is much clearer.
“Help more people” is not measurable.
“Enroll 100 participants in the program within twelve months” is measurable.
“Improve my finances” is too broad.
“Pay off $12,000 in debt within eighteen months” creates a clear outcome.
A strategic goal gives the project direction.
It prevents us from confusing activity with accomplishment.
Nehemiah had a clear result.
Complete the wall.
The wall was either finished or unfinished.
There was no ambiguity.
This kind of clarity helps people know what to prioritize.
Because when everything is important, nothing is truly important.
________________________________________
Coaching Moment
Many people struggle with goals because they set goals that are actually wishes.
“I want to be healthier.”
“I want to grow my business.”
“I want to write more.”
“I want to make a difference.”
Those statements express desire.
But they do not yet create accountability.
A strategic goal asks:
How much?
By when?
For whom?
What will be completed?
What evidence will show that the result has been achieved?

SLIDE 20
Milestone
What major checkpoint shows progress?
A milestone is a meaningful checkpoint along the journey.
It tells you that you are moving forward.
Milestones matter because large goals can feel distant.
When the finish line is months or years away, people can lose motivation.
Milestones make progress visible.
Suppose your goal is to launch a new program.
Your milestones might include:
Complete the curriculum.
Finish the website.
Recruit the first facilitators.
Launch the pilot group.
Receive participant feedback.
Enroll the first paying members.
Each checkpoint matters.
Each one confirms that the larger project is becoming real.
Esther's story contains a clear sequence of milestones.
She became queen.
She called for a fast.
She approached the king.
She held the first banquet.
She held the second banquet.
She exposed Haman.
Deliverance followed.
The final outcome did not happen in one moment.
There was a progression.
Each stage created the conditions for the next.
________________________________________
Important Principle
Milestones are not the final goal.
But they are evidence that the goal is becoming possible.
They also give us reasons to pause and acknowledge progress.
Some people never celebrate because they are always looking at what remains unfinished.
That can create exhaustion.
There is wisdom in stopping long enough to say:
“We are not finished, but we are farther than we were.”
________________________________________

Milestone three.
Do not list every task.
Choose the major checkpoints.

SLIDE 21
Production
The daily work that turns vision into reality
Production is where many people live every day.
Projects.
Tasks.
Deadlines.
Meetings.
Emails.
Habits.
Schedules.
But now we understand something important.
Production should not exist by itself.
Production should flow from action.
Action should flow from focus.
Focus should flow from identity.
When that connection is missing, people become busy but disconnected.
They may be productive, but not purposeful.
They may finish many things, but not the right things.
Production is not merely doing more.
It is doing what supports the larger mission.
Notice the statement on this slide:
Faithful obedience here accomplishes the larger mission.
That is powerful.
Because great missions are often fulfilled through very ordinary days.
Noah's ark was not completed through one dramatic moment.
It was completed through repeated construction.
Nehemiah's wall was not rebuilt through one speech.
It was rebuilt through organized labor.
Paul's churches were not established through one sermon.
They were built through travel, teaching, correction, mentoring, letter writing, prayer, and persistence.
The visible outcome came from repeated acts of faithfulness.
________________________________________
Personal Application
Your purpose may be enormous.
Your mission may affect thousands.
But today, it may look like:
Making one phone call.
Writing one page.
Submitting one application.
Preparing one lesson.
Helping one person.
Completing one difficult conversation.
Small does not mean insignificant.
Small actions become meaningful when they are connected to a larger purpose.

SLIDE 22
Project
What collection of work produces a result?
A project is a temporary body of work designed to produce something concrete.
Projects are smaller than the life-changing initiative.
They are the organized pieces that help deliver it.
Think of the life-changing project as the entire building.
Individual projects are the rooms, systems, and major components required to complete it.
Suppose your life-changing project is to launch a transformation academy.
The supporting projects might include:
Developing the curriculum.
Building the website.
Creating the enrollment process.
Recruiting instructors.
Producing the marketing materials.
Designing the participant workbook.
Each project has its own result.
Together, they advance the larger initiative.
This is what I call nested work.
A large mission contains major initiatives.
Major initiatives contain projects.
Projects contain milestones and tasks.
When work is organized this way, large dreams become manageable.
________________________________________
Coaching Insight
Many people feel overwhelmed because they keep looking at the entire vision.
They say:
“I have to build the whole thing.”
No.
You have to complete the next project.
Then the next one.
Then the next one.
Focus reduces overwhelm.

SLIDE 23
Task
What specific action must I complete?
A task is the smallest actionable work item.
This is the level of today.
A task should be clear enough that you know exactly what to do.
Not:
“Work on the program.”
Instead:
“Write the introduction to Module One.”
Not:
“Improve the website.”
Instead:
“Rewrite the homepage headline.”
Not:
“Contact people.”
Instead:
“Email the three potential facilitators by 3:00 p.m.”
A well-defined task reduces hesitation.
The brain resists vague work.
Clarity makes action easier.
Tasks accumulate into projects.
Projects advance goals.
Goals support life-changing initiatives.
Those initiatives fulfill assignments.
Assignments express mission.
Mission flows from purpose.
That means one small task can be connected all the way back to the reason you exist.
That is the heart of this hierarchy.

SLIDE 24
Habit & System
What repeated behavior sustains progress?
A task gets something done once.
A habit helps you continue.
A system makes consistent progress easier.
This distinction is critical.
A goal tells you where you want to go.
A system shapes what you repeatedly do.
For example:
The goal may be to write a book.
The system may be to write 500 words every weekday morning.
The goal may be to improve your health.
The system may be to walk thirty minutes after dinner and prepare meals on Sunday.
The goal may be to strengthen your spiritual life.
The system may be daily prayer, Scripture reading, journaling, and weekly accountability.
The goal may be to grow a business.
The system may include weekly follow-up, monthly financial review, scheduled content creation, and regular customer outreach.
Habits are the quiet engine beneath visible achievement.
People often admire the result but overlook the repetition that produced it.
They see the finished book.
They do not see the mornings of writing.
They see the successful organization.
They do not see the years of planning.
They see the strong leader.
They do not see the private disciplines.
________________________________________
Important Truth
No mission is fulfilled in one day.
It is fulfilled through what you do consistently.
You do not need to complete everything today.
But you do need a pattern that keeps you moving.
Consistency can accomplish what intensity cannot.
A person who works with excitement for three days and stops may accomplish less than someone who moves steadily for six months.
Faithfulness is powerful because it compounds.
________________________________________

A weekly planning session.
A daily prayer and reflection time.
Two follow-up calls each day.
A Friday review of goals and milestones.
Choose one habit.
Do not choose ten.
Start with the habit most directly connected to your current project.

SLIDE 25
A Task Without Purpose Feels Meaningless
A purpose without tasks never becomes reality
We have now walked through the full hierarchy.
We began with identity.
Then we moved into focus.
From focus, we moved into action.
And from action, we moved into production.
Now we can see why every level matters.
A task without purpose feels meaningless.
But a purpose without tasks never becomes reality.
That statement brings the entire framework together.
Some people have plenty of tasks.
They are busy every day.
They answer emails.
They attend meetings.
They solve problems.
They help other people.
They manage responsibilities.
But when those tasks are disconnected from purpose, even productive people can begin feeling empty.
They may ask:
“Why am I doing all of this?”
“Where is this leading?”
“Does any of this really matter?”
That is what happens when activity becomes disconnected from meaning.
But the opposite problem is just as serious.
Some people have powerful dreams.
They talk about purpose.
They talk about calling.
They talk about vision.
They talk about what God has shown them.
But they never build a schedule.
They never define a project.
They never establish a milestone.
They never complete the next task.
Purpose without execution becomes frustration.
At some point, vision must become work.
At some point, prayer must be followed by obedience.
At some point, the dream must enter the calendar.
________________________________________

Think of electricity.
A power plant may contain enormous energy.
But if that energy is not connected through wires, circuits, and outlets, it never reaches the lamp.
Purpose is the power.
Tasks are the connection.
Without purpose, tasks have no deeper energy.
Without tasks, purpose never reaches the world.
The hierarchy connects the two.
Meaning flows downward.
Reality builds upward.
Your purpose gives meaning to your mission.
Your mission gives direction to your projects.
Your projects give context to your tasks.
And your daily tasks gradually bring your purpose into visible reality.

SLIDE 26
The Biblical Pattern
The same sequence, every time
From Noah to Paul, the pattern remains remarkably consistent.
God forms the person.
Then He reveals a calling.
Then He gives an assignment.
That assignment produces significant work.
And the work is completed through daily obedience.
Let us say that sequence again.
God forms the person.
He reveals a calling.
He gives an assignment.
The person undertakes significant work.
Daily obedience completes the mission.
This order matters.
We often want God to begin with the visible work.
We want the opportunity.
The title.
The platform.
The influence.
The resources.
But God frequently begins in private.
He shapes character.
He tests motives.
He strengthens faith.
He develops patience.
He teaches obedience.
He forms the person before displaying the work.
David was formed in the field before he was recognized in the palace.
Joseph was formed in slavery and prison before he governed Egypt.
Moses was formed in the wilderness before confronting Pharaoh.
Paul was formed through surrender, study, ministry, opposition, and suffering.
Preparation is not punishment.
Hidden seasons are not wasted seasons.
God may be doing His deepest work in your life during a time when very little appears to be happening publicly.

You may feel as if your life has been moving too slowly.
Do not confuse invisibility with inactivity.
Roots grow in darkness.
Seeds develop underground.
Foundations are built before walls are seen.
God may be forming the person who can carry the assignment you have been praying to receive.

SLIDE 27
Seven Lives, One Pattern
The framework in Scripture
This chart brings together Noah, Joseph, David, Solomon, Nehemiah, Esther, and Paul.
Their stories were different.
Their personalities were different.
Their challenges were different.
Their assignments were different.
But the pattern remained.
Noah
Noah lived during a time of widespread corruption.
His cause was the broken condition of creation.
His calling was to build the ark.
His mission was to preserve life and protect God's covenant.
His life-changing project was the ark itself.
His goal was clear:
Finish before the flood.
Noah reminds us that obedience may require us to build something other people do not yet understand.
________________________________________
Joseph
Joseph's story was shaped by betrayal, slavery, false accusation, and imprisonment.
But the larger cause was preservation during famine.
His calling involved interpreting dreams and governing Egypt.
His mission was to prepare the nation.
His major project became a national grain-storage system.
His strategic goal was to store enough grain during seven years of abundance to survive seven years of famine.
Joseph teaches us that God may use painful preparation to position us for an assignment that saves others.
________________________________________
David
David's cause involved protecting God's people and honoring God's name.
His calling was kingship.
His mission was to establish and strengthen Israel.
His major initiative was unifying the nation.
His goal was to secure the kingdom.
But David's story also reminds us that a calling may be revealed long before it is fully realized.
He was anointed before he was appointed.
There was a long distance between the oil and the throne.
________________________________________
Solomon
Solomon's cause centered on wise leadership and worship.
His calling was to govern Israel.
His mission included building the Temple.
His life-changing project was one of the most significant construction projects in Israel's history.
His goal was to complete and dedicate the Temple.
Solomon shows us that one generation may prepare resources while another completes the work.
David prepared.
Solomon built.
Purpose often extends beyond one person.
________________________________________
Nehemiah
Nehemiah's cause began with grief over Jerusalem.
His calling was to return and lead restoration.
His mission was to rebuild the walls and restore the community.
His life-changing project was the wall.
His goal was measurable:
Complete it.
Nehemiah demonstrates how a burden can become a plan and how a plan can become visible restoration.
________________________________________
Esther
Esther faced the possible destruction of her people.
Her calling was to use her position and influence.
Her mission was to help save the Jewish people.
Her life-changing initiative involved exposing Haman and influencing the king.
Her goal was to reverse the threat.
Esther teaches us that position is not merely a privilege.
It is stewardship.
You may be where you are because someone else will need the courage God has developed in you.
________________________________________
Paul
Paul's cause was the spread of the Gospel.
His calling was to become an apostle to the Gentiles.
His mission involved planting churches and strengthening believers.
His life-changing work included missionary journeys, leadership development, and letters to the churches.
His goals included reaching strategic cities and establishing sustainable communities of faith.
Paul shows us that legacy can continue working long after the assignment appears complete.
________________________________________
Summary
Seven people.
Seven different contexts.
One recurring pattern.
A burden.
A calling.
A mission.
A major project.
A measurable goal.
And repeated obedience.
The lesson is not that everyone must build an ark, govern a nation, become royalty, rebuild a wall, or travel across an empire.
The lesson is that God connects ordinary obedience to extraordinary purpose.

SLIDE 28
Nehemiah: From Cause to Completion
Fifty-two days
Nehemiah heard that Jerusalem's walls were broken down.
His first response was not strategy.
It was grief.
He sat down.
He wept.
He fasted.
He prayed.
The cause touched his heart before it ever occupied his calendar.
But Nehemiah did not stop with emotion.
His burden became prayer.
His prayer became preparation.
His preparation became a request.
His request became permission.
Permission became inspection.
Inspection became organization.
Organization became construction.
Construction became completion.
That is transformation.
The wall was completed in fifty-two days.
But the fifty-two days did not begin when the workers picked up their tools.
The work began in Nehemiah's heart.
________________________________________
Milestone One: Permission granted
Nehemiah served the king as cupbearer.
He understood that moving forward required authorization.
So he prepared his request.
He identified what he needed.
He asked for time.
He asked for letters.
He asked for resources.
Faith did not make him careless.
It made him courageous and prepared.
________________________________________
Milestone Two: Survey the walls
When Nehemiah arrived, he did not immediately make a public announcement.
He inspected the damage.
He gathered facts.
He assessed reality.
Transformation requires honesty.
You cannot rebuild what you refuse to examine.
Before making a plan, Nehemiah looked carefully at what was broken.
________________________________________
Milestone Three: Organize the workers
Nehemiah did not attempt to rebuild the entire wall alone.
Families and groups were assigned sections.
The work became distributed.
This is a leadership principle.
A large vision requires shared ownership.
Great leaders do not merely inspire people.
They organize participation.
________________________________________
Milestone Four: Defeat opposition
The project faced ridicule.
Threats.
Internal conflict.
Fatigue.
Distraction.
False accusations.
But Nehemiah adjusted the strategy without abandoning the mission.
Some built while others guarded.
He responded to problems.
But he did not surrender focus.
Every life-changing project will face resistance.
Resistance does not always mean you are on the wrong path.
Sometimes resistance confirms that the work matters.
________________________________________
Milestone Five: Finish the walls
The wall was completed.
The visible result confirmed what had begun as an invisible burden.
Cause became calling.
Calling became mission.
Mission became project.
Project became goal.
Goal became milestones.
Milestones became daily tasks.
Daily tasks became completion.
That is the hierarchy in motion.
