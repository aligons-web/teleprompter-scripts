12-15 MINUTE TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT
OPENING
Have you ever spent a long time looking for something you lost?
Over the last several months, I have found myself searching for several things.
A phone.
Headphones.
A wallet.
A power tool.
At one point, even some lights that I still had not located.
And then there are other kinds of searches.
Searching for a solution to a technology problem.
Searching for an answer concerning a business project.
Searching for direction about something happening in life.
And I started thinking:
If I can become that focused when I am looking for a wallet, a phone, or some other possession… perhaps, it is the consequence that causes such intense search… replacing credit cards, closing or suspending your bank account, taking preventive measures to eliminate identity thief… how much more should I search for the wisdom of God? To pursue a guided life of purpose and alignment with God’s plan.
PAUSE
That question has stayed with me.
How strong is my search for wisdom?
Do I search for wisdom the way someone searches for treasure?
SCRIPTURE
Proverbs 2 tells us to seek wisdom like silver and search for it as hidden treasure. I recently watched a movie where three people were search for a treasure, but each person had issues with the other individuals while on their pursuit for the treasure. They couldn’t trust each other but they had to work together… ever had times you couldn’t trust others or yourself to do the right thing… and needed God? 
This group of disloyal treasure hunters had in common an energy for the pursuit.
Just as treasure has value, wisdom is valuable enough to pursue.
It requires persistence.
It requires digging.
It requires asking questions.
It requires stopping long enough to listen.
And it requires recognizing that God is teaching us through circumstances we did not choose.
CORE POINT 1
WISDOM CAN COME THROUGH THE UNEXPECTED.
First Corinthians 1 says that God has chosen what the world considers foolish to confound the wise.
A few days ago I wrote down another question:
How does God want to use me in ways that might confound the wise?
God has a history of working through things people would not expect (unexpected = things that comes as a surprise, not planned or foreseen). 
People we would not expect.
Circumstances we would not expect.
Methods we would not expect.
And sometimes the unexpected thing happening in our own life becomes the classroom in which God develops wisdom.
Have you ever gone through something and afterwards said:
Been there, done that… I learned something from that.
I will handle that differently next time.
I see something now that I could not see before.
That experience gave me insight.
Sometimes we call those difficult experiences trials.
James chapter 1:2-6 tells believers what their focus should be with trials… to count it joy when we encounter various trials because the testing of our faith produces endurance.
And then James makes an interesting connection.
He begins talking about trials and endurance, and only a few verses later he says:
If anyone lacks wisdom, let him ask God.
Why? This is the most critical thing that is lacking as created beings for God’s purposes… we do not understand the workings of God.
Because sometimes we need wisdom to understand what God is doing while we are going through something we did not expect.
QUESTION
Could something you currently consider an interruption actually be producing insight?
Could something you consider an obstacle be developing endurance?
Could something you are asking God to remove also contain something God wants you to learn?
PAUSE
That does not mean every bad circumstance came directly from God.
But it does mean God is able to teach us and mature us in the middle of circumstances we did not choose.
CORE POINT 2
BIBLICAL WISDOM IS MORE THAN INTELLIGENCE.
I began asking myself another question.
What exactly is wisdom?
We often think wisdom simply means knowing a lot.
But biblical wisdom goes deeper.
Proverbs 9:10 says:
The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom.
That means wisdom begins with our relationship and posture toward God.
Reverence.
Honor.
Submission.
Awareness.
Biblical wisdom includes knowing what to do, but also understanding whose direction we are following.
A person can be technically intelligent and still make foolish decisions.
A person can know business.
Technology.
Education.
Finances.
Leadership.
Even theology.
And still fail to live wisely.
Biblical wisdom connects knowledge to God.
One definition I wrote in my journal was:
Wisdom is knowledge of God's plans and purposes and the ability to live accordingly.
Another way I wrote it was simply:
My Bible resources put it this way… “The foundation of wisdom is fearing the Lord, and as you honor and esteem/respect God, live in awe of His power, and obey His Word, His wisdom becomes yours as you allow Him to become the controlling influence in your life.  is living well. Wisdom comes as you develop God-awareness and walk with Him, growing a more conscious, worshipful attitude. 
Knowing what to do.
Understanding the limits of my own understanding.
And maintaining the proper relationship with God.
CORE POINT 3
WISDOM MUST MOVE FROM INFORMATION TO TRANSFORMATION.
James later describes another person.
A person who hears the Word but does not do it.
James says that person is like someone looking into a mirror, seeing himself, walking away, and forgetting what he saw.
That challenged me personally.
Because it is possible to prepare a Bible lesson.
Teach a Bible lesson.
Preach a message.
Quote Scripture.
And still fail to allow that same Word to govern the way I respond in everyday situations.
SCRIPTURE
James 1:22 says:
Be doers of the Word, and not hearers only.
So I began examining myself.
I used the word EXAMINE to start asking some questions.
What do I put my ENERGY into?
What do I need to EXCLUDE from my life?
What do I need to ACCELERATE?
What should MOTIVATE me?
Where do I need greater INTENTIONALITY?
Who should be NOTICED most in my identity?
And where should my EFFORT be directed?
For me, the answer keeps coming back to this:
My life must become increasingly aligned with the principles of God for his created beings.
Not simply my teaching.
My life.
My conversations.
My reactions.
My priorities.
My time.
My relationships.
My decisions.
My work.
CORE POINT 4
TRIALS CAN REVEAL WHETHER WISDOM IS BECOMING MATURITY.
James says that endurance should complete its work so that we become mature and complete.
That changes how we look at adversity.
Some trials may involve sickness.
Being overlooked.
Unfairness.
Difficult relationships.
Career frustrations.
A declining organization or ministry.
A business idea that is not developing as quickly as expected.
A family situation.
Financial pressure.
Or simply trying to determine what season of life God is leading you into next.
The natural question is:
How do I get out of this?
But wisdom sometimes asks another question:
God, what are You developing in me while I am going through this?
I remember in 2020 when we were experiencing a pandemic… it was an uneasy time, and I remember making the most of the time completing a book and producing a virtual Bible study. A few years later, I completed a doctoral degree. That was 6 years ago and things are back to normal, but what will your life become in the next 6 years when it is 2032?
Do we realize that if we do not have challenges, difficulties, friction points, and conflicts (trials), our lives would continue in a norm that isn’t impacted to change… would I have started a virtual Bible study or completed my first book without a pandemic? I know that the multiple degrees I completed was directly related to a high level of career discontentment. 
PAUSE
Wise people begin learning to identify the nature of their trials and understand how they should respond to them.
And that is where wisdom, faith, endurance, and maturity begin to connect.
CORE POINT 5
PURSUING WISDOM REQUIRES INTENTIONAL SEARCHING.
Think again about searching for treasure.
People hunting for treasure study clues.
They investigate.
They keep looking.
They overcome obstacles.
They sometimes work with other people.
They refuse to quit because they believe what they are searching for has value.
Proverbs 4:7 says wisdom is the principal thing, so get wisdom.
What would happen if we approached God's wisdom with that level of determination?
Every day:
Search God's Word.
Ask God questions.
Pray.
Listen to the Holy Spirit.
Learn from wise people.
Reflect on our experiences.
Pay attention to patterns.
And ask:
God, what are You showing me?
QUESTION FOR AUDIENCE
What are you currently searching for?
Direction?
Purpose?
A career change?
A solution?
Healing in a relationship?
A business opportunity?
A ministry assignment?
Clarity about your next chapter?
Now ask a second question.
Instead of only asking God for the answer, what wisdom might God want to develop in you during the search?
CLOSING
I have become increasingly convinced that wisdom is one of the greatest resources God gives us for navigating life.
But we cannot merely admire wisdom.
We have to pursue it.
We cannot merely hear God's Word.
We have to practice it.
And we cannot assume every unexpected season means we are outside of God's purpose.
Sometimes the unexpected becomes the place where wisdom develops.
So this week I want to leave you with three questions.
What am I searching for?
What might God be teaching me through what I am experiencing?
And what would obedience to God's Word look like in this situation?
PAUSE
Maybe that is where transformation starts.
Not simply knowing something new.
But becoming different because of what God has shown us.
END OF 12-MINUTE CORE
========================================
OPTIONAL 2-3 MINUTE EXTENSION
If time permits, connect this to purpose.
Sometimes we want God to immediately show us our purpose, assignment, business, ministry, or next project.
But God may be developing the person before fully revealing the assignment.
Joseph had dreams before he had responsibility.
David was anointed before he became king.
Moses spent years outside Egypt before returning to his assignment.
Nehemiah carried a burden before rebuilding Jerusalem.
Esther entered a position before understanding the significance of her placement.
Paul experienced a dramatic interruption before beginning a completely different life assignment.
So perhaps another question is:
What if the season you are currently in is preparing you for something you cannot completely see yet?
That is why wisdom matters.
Wisdom helps us understand not only where we want to go, but how God wants us to live while we are becoming the person capable of going there.

LIFE ARCHETYPE QUIZ — STARTER CONCEPT
Instead of building the entire quiz immediately, introduce four preliminary archetypes during your live session.
THE EXPLORER
I know something needs to change, but I am still trying to understand myself and my direction.
THE BUILDER
I have an idea or direction and need structure, goals, milestones, and consistent action.
THE TRANSFORMER
I am actively changing old patterns, beliefs, habits, relationships, or circumstances.
THE IMPLEMENTER
I have greater clarity and now need to turn that clarity into a project, service, ministry, business, or measurable outcome.
Sample live question:

A. I am searching for clarity.
B. I know where I want to go but need a plan.
